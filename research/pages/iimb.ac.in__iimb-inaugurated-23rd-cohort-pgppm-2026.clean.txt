# IIM Bangalore inaugurates 23rd cohort of the Post Graduate Programme in Public Policy and Management (PGPPM)
{IMG /sites/default/files/2026-08/Students.jpg}
The Chief Guest, Dr. Sweta C Saxena, former Director, Gender, Poverty and Social Policy Division, United Nations Economic Commission, underscores policy beyond economic growth
August 17, 2026, Bangalore: IIM Bangalore inaugurated the 23rd cohort of its [https://www.iimb.ac.in/programmes/pgppm] Post Graduate Programme in Public Policy & Management (PGPPM) , welcoming 30 participants from government and non-government backgrounds to the 14-month master's programme via hybrid mode. The flagship initiative of IIMB’s [https://www.iimb.ac.in/centres-of-excellence/cpp] Centre for Public Policy (CPP) continues its legacy of shaping leaders who can navigate India’s governance challenges with clarity, innovation, and empathy.
This year's cohort brings together officers from the IAS, IPS, revenue services (direct and indirect tax), statistical services, and healthcare administration, alongside professionals from the banking, finance, and social sectors.
Delivering the welcome address, Prof. [https://www.iimb.ac.in/user/105/arnab-mukherji] Arnab Mukherji , Chairperson, PGPPM, welcomed the 23rd cohort and traced the programme's evolution since 2002, noting that its alumni have gone on to work with institutions ranging from the World Bank to other international bodies. He spoke about how, in 2026, the boundary between the state and the private sector is changing, not only in terms of sectors, but in the very functions that today's public policy professionals are expected to perform. He described the programme as designed to address that very complexity, with a cross-functional approach built around a shared idea the programme calls the "problem narrative", the notion that the office a person holds shapes how they approach solving a policy problem.
"Public policy today is no longer confined within the boundary of a single department or sector; it demands a cross-functional management lens. That is precisely the complexity this programme is designed to address," said Prof. Arnab.
Prof. [https://www.iimb.ac.in/user/70/u-dinesh-kumar] U Dinesh Kumar , Director-in-charge, IIM Bangalore, delivered the inaugural address, congratulating the incoming cohort and emphasising the importance of artificial intelligence in transforming governance and public service delivery, from healthcare access through frontline workers to reducing the cost of public services, while cautioning that its adoption must be balanced against its impact on employment.
Setting out the policy context at IIMB, Prof. Gopal Naik, Chairperson, Centre for Public Policy (CPP), traced the Centre's founding in 2000 by the Government of India to build policy analysis and implementation capacity within government, against the backdrop of the 1991 economic reforms and the wave of globalisation and new public management reforms of the 1990s. He noted CPP's role in pioneering public policy education in India, including its mid-career training programmes for IAS officers, its PhD programme, and its annual international conference on public policy and management, which draws around 200 researchers each year.
The Chief Guest, Dr. Sweta C Saxena, former Director, Gender, Poverty and Social Policy Division, United Nations Economic Commission for Africa, Addis Ababa, was introduced to the cohort by Dr. Anjana Dube, Chief Policy Officer, CPP, IIMB, who noted Dr. Saxena's 25-year unbroken record of research publications, spanning peer-reviewed papers, book chapters, and flagship reports. Dr. Saxena has also held senior positions at the International Monetary Fund, UNESCAP, and the Bank for International Settlements, and taught for nine years at the University of Pittsburgh.
Keynote Address
Delivering the keynote, titled ‘ The Pathway from Economic Growth to Human Resilience through the Instrumentality of Public Policy,’ Dr. Sweta Saxena structured her address around three questions for the incoming cohort: Why choose public policy, how does one influence policy, and what does one do with that influence? illustrating each from her own research and field experience.
On the question of why, she drew on her doctoral research on the Asian financial crisis to argue that understanding why economies and policies fail is a prerequisite to designing policies that make them more resilient.
On how to influence policy, she pointed to her research on post-crisis recovery patterns, spanning data from 190 countries, which found that output losses following financial and political shocks are often permanent rather than temporary, a phenomenon economists call hysteresis. She also argued that GDP alone is an incomplete measure of national progress, since it captures the size of an economy but not how growth is distributed or its impact on wellbeing.
“GDP can tell you the size of the pie, but it says nothing about how the pie is shared. Public Policy must have a wider impact; it should reach everybody, not just a few”
And finally, on what to do with that influence , she shared how her research at UNESCAP helped shape the "pro-Planet, pro-People, pro-Profit" and "Lifestyle for Environment (LiFE)" concepts referenced in national policy addresses, as a successful example of research translating into policy. She balanced this with an account of her field experience in South Sudan, where she worked to help stabilise the country's economy amid an oil revenue dispute with Sudan, as an example of the real-world constraints and institutional limits that can shape and sometimes limit a policy professional's ability to effect change.
Proposing the vote of thanks, Archana Srivastava, PGPPM Office, introduced the profile of the incoming batch, comprising participants from both government and non-government backgrounds, and thanked the Chief Administrative Office, the Admissions Office, the Marketing and Communications teams, and the PGPPM office staff and faculty members for their contribution to organising the induction.
