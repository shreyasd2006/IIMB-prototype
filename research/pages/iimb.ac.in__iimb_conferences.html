    Research & Publications Office to host seminar on ‘Does Bank Lending to Nonbanks Affect Credit Allocation and Systemic Risk?’

Add to Calendar 2026-10-22 05:30:00 2026-10-16 10:08:06 Research & Publications Office to host seminar on ‘Does Bank Lending to Nonbanks Affect Credit Allocation and Systemic Risk?’ Talk by Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj, Indian School of Business, Hyderabad 13 October 2026, Bengaluru: The Office of Research and Publications at IIM Bangalore will host a research seminar titled ‘Does Bank Lending to Nonbanks Affect Credit Allocation and Systemic Risk?’, featuring Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj, Assistant Professor of Finance at the Indian School of Business (ISB), Hyderabad. The seminar will be held on 22 October 2026, at 12:00 pm, in Classroom Q-001 at IIMB. Abstract The study examines the causal effects of bank lending to non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) using administrative loan-level data. Exploiting a regulatory reform that eased capital requirements for bank exposures to NBFIs, the study finds that increased bank funding enables NBFIs to expand credit to smaller, younger, and riskier firms, thereby promoting financial inclusion and allocative efficiency. However, evidence from the failure of a large NBFI reveals that bank–NBFI linkages can also amplify systemic risk. Banks exposed to the failed institution reduce lending to unrelated firms and NBFIs, which, in turn, curtail lending to their own borrowers, propagating credit contractions through the financial system. The findings highlight the dual role of bank–NBFI linkages: while they expand credit access and improve credit allocation, they can also transmit financial distress through interconnected lending relationships. Speaker Profile Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj is an Assistant Professor of Finance at the Indian School of Business. He holds a PhD in Finance from the NYU Stern School of Business. His research examines how financial institutions shape credit allocation, financial stability, and real economic outcomes, with a focus on banking, private equity, and the growing role of non-bank financial institutions in modern financial systems. Another stream of his research explores how firms adapt to climate change and natural disasters, particularly in relation to employment, investment, and productivity. His research has been published or accepted in leading finance and economics journals, including the Review of Financial Studies, Journal of Political Economy: Macroeconomics, and Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis. His work has received research grants from organisations such as the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) and has been presented at leading academic, policy, and industry forums. He teaches corporate finance at the graduate level. Prof. Bhardwaj also serves as an Independent Director at Zerodha Trustee Private Limited, focusing on governance, risk oversight, and regulatory issues associated with the growth of passive investing and retail participation in financial markets. Prior to entering academia, he worked at Deutsche Bank, where he engaged with financial markets and hedge fund clients. His research combines large-scale data with modern empirical methods to address questions relevant to regulators, financial institutions, investors, and policymakers, bridging academic research, financial practice, and public policy.     GIFT Finance Olympiad: National Student Case Challenge 2026

Add to Calendar 2026-11-28 05:30:00 2026-10-16 10:08:06 GIFT Finance Olympiad: National Student Case Challenge 2026 Organized by the Centre for Capital Markets and Risk Management (CCMRM) at IIM Bangalore, in collaboration with GIFT City 14 October, 2026, Bengaluru: The Centre for Capital Markets and Risk Management (CCMRM) at IIM Bangalore, in collaboration with GIFT City, invites postgraduate students across India to participate in the GIFT Finance Olympiad: National Student Case Challenge 2026. The competition offers students an opportunity to engage with a real-world case drawn from GIFT City’s financial infrastructure and develop practical, evidence-based solutions to questions shaping India’s international financial markets. Registrations are open from 14th October 2026. Interested students are requested to review the instructions below and complete the registration using the following link: CCMRM, IIM Bangalore  × GIFT City Finance Competition 2026 – Fill in form The competition will unfold in three stages: a national online case challenge, in-person regional finals in Bengaluru, Delhi, Kolkata and Mumbai, and a Grand Finale at GIFT City, Gandhinagar. Through this progression, participants will have the opportunity to apply their academic learning, examine real market and institutional trade-offs, and present and defend their recommendations before a panel of academic and industry experts. About the Competition GIFT City’s International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) is emerging as a gateway for offshore banking, capital markets, aircraft leasing, fund management and sustainable finance. As its institutions evolve and engage with global financial centres, questions of market design, product innovation, governance and institutional strategy become increasingly important. The GIFT Finance Olympiad invites students to examine these questions through an applied case centred on GIFT City’s financial ecosystem. Participants are expected to go beyond conceptual solutions and assess the commercial rationale, technical feasibility, implementation challenges and risks associated with their recommendations. The emphasis is on sound analysis, independent thinking and solutions that strengthen GIFT IFSC’s long-term credibility and competitiveness. Eligibility The competition is open to postgraduate students enrolled in any recognized college, university or B-school in India, irrespective of discipline. Each team must comprise four students, with interdisciplinary teams particularly encouraged. Students from finance, economics, technology, data and related fields are welcome to collaborate. Each student may participate in only one team. Why participate? Engage with a live, real-world question facing India's offshore financial centre. Present before exchange officials, bankers and academics. Interact with practitioners from GIFT IFSC institutions and IIM Bangalore faculty. Compete for cash prizes and national recognition. City winners will advance to the Grand Finale at GIFT City, Gandhinagar. Competition Format The competition will be conducted in three stages. Stage I: National Case Challenge (Online) Teams will solve a real-world GIFT City financial infrastructure case. Teams will choose one of two analytical tracks. Each team will submit a deck comprising one title slide and up to four content slides. Submissions will be evaluated by a panel comprising academics and industry practitioners. Stage II: Regional Finals (In-Person) Regional Finals will be held in four cities. Shortlisted teams will compete in a single-day, in-person round comprising a case extension and a live presentation with judge Q&A. The winning team from each regional city will advance to the Grand Finale at GIFT City. 21st November 2026 – Delhi 28th November 2026 – Bengaluru 30th November 2026 – Kolkata 5th December 2026 – Mumbai Stage III: Grand Finale – GIFT City, Gandhinagar The four regional winners will be invited to GIFT City, Gandhinagar for the Grand Finale. Finalist teams will receive a fresh, more detailed case extension on the same theme, to be solved under time constraints and presented live to the judging panel, including a Q&A. The date of the Grand Finale will be announced separately. Analytical Tracks Track 1 – Markets & Product Design GIFT Nifty has successfully consolidated offshore trading interest in Nifty index derivatives onto an IFSC platform. Building on this, suppose NSE IX is evaluating the launch of single-stock futures on a basket of large Indian companies (via depository receipt-linked structures) for trading by global investors out of GIFT IFSC. Your proposal should clearly identify and justify: Target participants and eligibility (FPIs, NRIs, global institutions). Contract design (underlying, lot size, settlement type – cash vs physical). Clearing, margining and counterparty risk framework. Liquidity-provision mechanism (market makers, incentives). Interoperability with onshore Indian markets and existing GIFT Nifty infrastructure. Operational challenges and expected benefits to GIFT IFSC's positioning. Track 2 – Institutional Strategy & Governance Global exchanges, custodians and clearing corporations are evaluating deeper partnerships, cross-listing arrangements or equity stakes in GIFT IFSC market infrastructure institutions. Develop a governance and strategy framework for how a GIFT IFSC exchange or clearing corporation should evaluate and structure such partnerships. Your framework should consider: Strategic rationale (liquidity access, technology, credibility) versus risks of dependence. Governance structure and board representation for foreign partners. Data-sharing and cybersecurity considerations. Competitive positioning against Singapore Exchange, Nasdaq Dubai and Hong Kong Exchanges. Long-term sustainability of the partnership beyond an initial liquidity boost. Exit/renegotiation provisions and accountability mechanisms. What will the jury look for? Teams should evaluate not only whether their proposed solution is commercially and technically feasible, but also whether it strengthens GIFT IFSC's long-term credibility as a financial centre. Does the proposed expansion address a genuine market need, or merely chase volume/headlines? Is the proposed approach necessary, or could the same objective be achieved with a simpler design? Do the anticipated benefits justify the additional risks taken on? Under what conditions would the proposal strengthen investor trust, market integrity and GIFT IFSC's global standing over the next decade? Teams should clearly state all assumptions underlying their recommendations and justify the rationale for their proposed design choices. Evaluation criterion Weight Quality and originality of analysis 30% Depth of market or institutional strategy thinking (per chosen track) 25% Feasibility and implementability of recommendations 20% Understanding of market and strategic trade-offs 15% Clarity and quality of presentation 10% AI Use Disclosure Teams may use AI tools to support research and drafting. However, submissions must include a brief disclosure slide or footnote identifying the tools used and explaining how they were used. Submissions that rely on AI-generated analysis without genuine engagement with GIFT City-specific facts, figures and trade-offs will score poorly against the evaluation criteria, particularly on originality and feasibility. Selection for the regional stage will further test teams' ability to defend and extend their own analysis live, in person. Interested students should register through the official registration form and submit their final PDF presentation in accordance with the instructions published on the competition webpage. Official case and competition page: The official CCMRM webpage will serve as the central source for the complete case, submission guidelines, registration details and competition updates. Workshop Overview The Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is designed to give scholars developmental feedback on sustainability-related International Business (IB) research. It offers a supportive environment in which participants receive constructive comments from experienced researchers, helping them strengthen their manuscripts for submission to leading IB journals such as the Journal of International Business Studies, Journal of World Business, Global Strategy Journal and Journal of International Management. Sustainability has become a central theme in IB research, spanning climate change, ESG, sustainable global value chains, responsible internationalization, the circular economy, social inclusion, energy transition, biodiversity and the Sustainable Development Goals. South Asia is an important setting for such work because of its rapidly growing economies, institutional diversity, environmental vulnerabilities, expanding multinational activity and increasing integration into global value chains. Many scholars, particularly doctoral students and early-career researchers, have limited opportunities to receive detailed feedback before submitting to journals. The workshop addresses this gap through a structured mentoring environment in which participants receive in-depth comments from senior scholars with expertise in sustainability and International Business. Who Should Apply The workshop welcomes submissions from doctoral students and faculty researching sustainability-related IB topics. Participants attending the ISC Conference and the AIB South Asia Chapter Conference may also submit the same paper to this PDW. Both conceptual and empirical papers at various stages of development are welcome. Please note: Valid AIB Membership at the time of the event is required.  Relevant topics include, but are not limited to, climate change and international business, ESG and corporate sustainability, sustainable global value chains, and responsible internationalization. They also include emerging market multinationals and sustainability, sustainable innovation, the circular economy, green supply chains, and sustainable finance. Further themes are institutional environments and sustainability, informality and sustainable development, inclusive business models, sustainable entrepreneurship, and digital technologies for sustainability. Workshop Format The workshop will follow a roundtable format in which participants are organised into small groups, each with one mentor and four to five paper presenters. Group composition may change depending on the number of submissions and mentors. Each paper will receive approximately 25 to 30 minutes of discussion. The author will begin with a brief presentation of five to seven minutes, followed by detailed developmental comments from the mentor for 12 to 15 minutes. The session will close with an interactive discussion with participants for eight to ten minutes. Submission Process Applicants are required to submit an extended abstract of two to three pages or a full working paper, along with a short statement describing the stage of the research and the specific feedback they seek. Note: Submissions should be emailed to sandeep.yadav@iimb.ac.in and sustainabilitysig@aib.world, with the subject line "Submission for AIB Sustainability SIG PDW". Submissions will be evaluated on their relevance to sustainability and International Business, originality, potential academic contribution, and potential for constructive development through the workshop.
