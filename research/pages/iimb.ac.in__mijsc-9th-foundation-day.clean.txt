# Strengthening synergies between India & Japan: Mizuho India Japan Study Centre hosts 9th Foundation Day celebrations
{IMG /sites/default/files/2026-09/Publications%20release.jpg}
Addresses by global industry and academic leaders, release of publications and cultural program were highlights of the event themed on, ‘Connect, Explore, Develop’
18 September, 2026, Bengaluru: “India and Japan working together in academics, research, industrial and other projects can be beneficial for both the countries and create happiness for a large number of people”, observed Prof. [https://www.iimb.ac.in/user/70/u-dinesh-kumar] U Dinesh Kumar , Director-in-charge, IIM Bangalore, during the ninth Foundation Day celebrations of the [https://www.iimb.ac.in/mijsc/] Mizuho India Japan Study Centre (MIJSC) , an IIMB Centre of Excellence, earlier this evening. The event, themed on, ‘Connect, Explore, Develop’, apart from celebrating the Centre’s key milestones, brought together industry and academic leaders from across the globe for insightful discussions.
Prof. Dinesh Kumar delivered an engaging address that underscored the deep cultural ties between India and Japan, and emphasized the complementarity of the two nations in the economic as well as social development spheres. Highlighting the role collaboration can play in the progress of any nation, Prof. Dinesh Kumar hailed MIJSC for playing a crucial part in strengthening Indo-Japan bilateral ties through academic research, executive education as well as cultural exchange. “During the same celebrations next year, I am sure we will have more projects and collaborative initiatives to boast of.”
Indo-Japan cooperation: Driving energy efficiency, technology transfer, regional security and mutual growth
This year’s MIJSC Foundation Day celebrations featured two luminaries – Guest of Honour Prof. Tatsuya Terazawa, Chairman and CEO, The Institute of Energy Economics, Japan (IEEJ) and keynote speaker Kinji Saito, Ex Director and Councilor, Chief Global Marketing, Suzuki Motor Corporation.
Prof. Tatsuya Terazawa discussed the complex issue of the Energy Trilemma plaguing the world today. “It is very challenging to achieve the objectives of energy security, energy equity and environmental sustainability, simultaneously”, he lamented. He spoke about issues arising out of the current geopolitical situation, wars, sanctions, etc. and added that there existed a conflict between energy security and energy affordability.
Explaining that Japan has been striving to boost the Asian energy framework, he said, “But Japan alone cannot achieve such a massive goal. High-level dialogue and initiatives are under way, and India and Japan together have the potential to enhance energy efficiency in the region. Solar power, batteries and automobiles are some of the sub-sectors that the two countries could work on, in partnership. Energy Trilemma is becoming more and more complex by the day, and India-Japan are uniquely positioned to be excellent partners in resolving this dilemma. And MIJSC can act as the core of developing this partnership to overcome such challenges.”
Kinji Saito provided an overview of the opportunities and challenges of India-Japan cooperation, with special focus on Suzuki Motor Corporation’s ‘Next Bharat Ventures’, an initiative aiming to invest in and mentor early-stage entrepreneurs in India’s rural/semi-urban markets. Pointing out that this initiative of Suzuki aligns with India's national vision of Viksit Bharat 2047, he said that India’s population and the country’s very evolved Digital Public Infrastructure provides a plethora of opportunities for that level of investment. “Japan seeks to nurture India’s impact entrepreneurs, who are succeeding due to a scientific approach to social issues and being digital by default. In fact, under the Next Bharat Fund, we initially put 40 million USD in 2024, which we increased to 200 million USD in 2026, due to our confidence in India and its huge potential for growth. The investments may go up even further in the future. This will result in every stretch of India being developed. So, Viksit Bharat will not just be a dream – it will be a reality!”
“Suzuki’s way is to make many SMEs, not just one unicorn. SMEs will grow successfully and in good speed. Specific schemes have uplifted Indian rural economy, aiding farmers, artisans, tribal farmers, waste pickers, etc. Offerings like eBik for mobility, Blackfrog for healthcare, Plutas AI powering insurance and real-time climate risk management, are a few such measures. Indian and Japanese SMEs and large corporations are joining the opportunity of Next Billion to unlock a shared investment and growth journey.”
Earlier in the evening, Prof. [https://www.iimb.ac.in/user/251/jaideep-sarkar] Jaideep Sarkar , Chairperson, Mizuho India Japan Study Centre and faculty of the Entrepreneurship area, while delivering the welcome address, highlighted the increased pace of activities and initiatives between Indian and Japanese universities. “We already have useful collaborations with the Waseda, Hitotsubashi and Keio universities; in the future we will be aiming for more industrial clusters.”
“India has gained tremendously from Japanese investments, etc. Japan too, stands to gain from Indian scientific, engineering, software, academic and intellectual prowess and manpower. Marrying Japan’s hi-tech capability with India’s frugal manufacturing will leverage the strengths of both the countries. India and Japan have announced around 120 cooperation agreements earlier this year, and now we have to ensure sustainability of such missions. We need knowledge about Japanese business practices to successfully navigate these surging bilateral investments and initiatives. India and MIJSC are poised to take firm steps towards meeting that goal.”
Dr. Saideep Rathnam , Chief Operating Officer, Mizuho India Japan Study Centre, set the context of the event and delivered the vote of thanks. “The next cycle of improvement begins with today’s event. We need to continue to work together, learn from each other and create something new and beneficial for all”, he said. Talking about the future roadmap for the Centre, he announced that with its various initiatives, the Centre is poised to evolve from its mission of ‘Connect, Explore, Develop’ to ‘Understand, Connect and Co-create’.
Other highlights
The evening also included a milestone video showcasing MIJSC’s progress over the past year emphasizing its commitment to fostering India-Japan relations , and the release of publications by Prof. [https://www.iimb.ac.in/user/106/sourav-mukherji] Sourav Mukherji , Dean, Faculty and faculty of the Organizational Behavior & Human Resources Management area of IIMB along with special guests and dignitaries, which included a coffee table book, 5th volume of a monograph, the Kizuna Sabha Volume 2 and brochures, followed by a musical performance in collaboration with the Indo-Japanese Chamber of Commerce and Industry, Karnataka, by the group ‘Black Box’.
The Mizuho India Japan Study Centre aims to promote understanding of Japan in India and India in Japan through research, teaching and policy outreach efforts. The Centre facilitates collaborative initiatives among academia, industry and government agencies of both the countries to work on areas of mutual interest.
