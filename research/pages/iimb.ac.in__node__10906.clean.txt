# Research & Publications Office to host seminar on ‘Does Bank Lending to Nonbanks Affect Credit Allocation and Systemic Risk?’
Talk by Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj, Indian School of Business, Hyderabad
13 October 2026, Bengaluru: The Office of Research and Publications at IIM Bangalore will host a research seminar titled ‘Does Bank Lending to Nonbanks Affect Credit Allocation and Systemic Risk?’, featuring Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj, Assistant Professor of Finance at the Indian School of Business (ISB), Hyderabad. The seminar will be held on 22 October 2026, at 12:00 pm, in Classroom Q-001 at IIMB.
Abstract
The study examines the causal effects of bank lending to non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) using administrative loan-level data. Exploiting a regulatory reform that eased capital requirements for bank exposures to NBFIs, the study finds that increased bank funding enables NBFIs to expand credit to smaller, younger, and riskier firms, thereby promoting financial inclusion and allocative efficiency.
However, evidence from the failure of a large NBFI reveals that bank–NBFI linkages can also amplify systemic risk. Banks exposed to the failed institution reduce lending to unrelated firms and NBFIs, which, in turn, curtail lending to their own borrowers, propagating credit contractions through the financial system.
The findings highlight the dual role of bank–NBFI linkages: while they expand credit access and improve credit allocation, they can also transmit financial distress through interconnected lending relationships.
Speaker Profile
Prof. Abhishek Bhardwaj is an Assistant Professor of Finance at the Indian School of Business. He holds a PhD in Finance from the NYU Stern School of Business.
His research examines how financial institutions shape credit allocation, financial stability, and real economic outcomes, with a focus on banking, private equity, and the growing role of non-bank financial institutions in modern financial systems. Another stream of his research explores how firms adapt to climate change and natural disasters, particularly in relation to employment, investment, and productivity.
His research has been published or accepted in leading finance and economics journals, including the Review of Financial Studies , Journal of Political Economy: Macroeconomics , and Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis . His work has received research grants from organisations such as the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) and has been presented at leading academic, policy, and industry forums. He teaches corporate finance at the graduate level.
Prof. Bhardwaj also serves as an Independent Director at Zerodha Trustee Private Limited, focusing on governance, risk oversight, and regulatory issues associated with the growth of passive investing and retail participation in financial markets. Prior to entering academia, he worked at Deutsche Bank, where he engaged with financial markets and hedge fund clients.
His research combines large-scale data with modern empirical methods to address questions relevant to regulators, financial institutions, investors, and policymakers, bridging academic research, financial practice, and public policy.
